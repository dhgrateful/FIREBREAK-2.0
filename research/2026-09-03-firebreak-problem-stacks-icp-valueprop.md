# FIREBREAK — Industry Problem Stacks, ICP & Value Prop (Hormozi method)

Foundation for every outreach email. **Method (Alex Hormozi):** lead with the
prospect's single most painful, most *active* problem (the "burning platform"),
agitate the cost of inaction, then present FIREBREAK as the one thing that removes it.
No competitor comparison — this is a **new category** (fight fire with directed **air**,
not water or chemicals). Rank = urgency 1–10 (10 = burning now). 🔥 = actively
escalating / deadline-driven RIGHT NOW = lead with these.

**The one big domino (cross-cutting wedge):** FIREBREAK uses **no water, no chemicals,
no foam**. The single fastest-moving problem across 3 of the 4 industries is the
**AFFF/PFAS firefighting-foam ban + "water is a liability."** That is the spearhead.
Second wedge: **"it works when and where water can't."**

---

## 1) PRIVATE INDUSTRY (high-hazard facilities with an on-site brigade)

**ICP:** refineries, petrochemical & chemical plants, LNG terminals, gas plants, tank
farms, bulk fuel storage, utilities/substations — sites with their own fire brigade or
emergency response team. **Buyers:** Fire Chief / Emergency Response Lead; Process
Safety / EHS Manager; Operations / Risk / Facilities Director.

| # | Problem (the pain they feel) | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔥 **AFFF/PFAS foam ban & liability** — legacy firefighting foam is being banned/restricted; disposal, testing, and litigation costs mounting. They need suppression with **zero chemicals**. | 10 |
| 2 | 🔥 **The first 5–10 minutes** — a fire at a process unit/tank farm escalates before mutual aid arrives; the brigade must hold or redirect it *now*. | 9 |
| 3 | **Water is a liability** near energized equipment, reactive chemicals, and live/hot process — water spreads hydrocarbon fire, risks reactions, creates runoff. | 9 |
| 4 | **Unit-to-unit cascade** — one unit reaching the next tank/unit turns an incident into a multi-million-dollar shutdown. | 8 |
| 5 | **Firewater/agent supply is finite** — deluge pressure, foam stock, hydrant capacity run out mid-event. | 8 |
| 6 | 🔥 **Rising regulatory + insurer pressure** — PSM/RMP audits, API standards, shrinking insurance capacity, premiums up; must prove layered response. | 7 |
| 7 | **Firefighter exposure & liability** — brigade members in close; LODD, workers' comp, litigation. | 7 |
| 8 | **Environmental runoff / cleanup** — contaminated firewater, PFAS fines, ESG exposure. | 6 |
| 9 | **Downtime cost** — unplanned outage runs into millions per day. | 6 |
| 10 | **Aging assets + climate extremes** — more ignition risk under hotter, harsher conditions. | 5 |

**Lead emails with:** #1 (no chemicals), #2 (own the first minutes), #3 (hold a line
without water). **Value prop:** *"Another layer between an ignition and a shutdown —
one that runs on air, so you can hold or redirect flame near live process without adding
water or foam to the problem."*

## 2) PRESCRIBED BURNS (anyone running controlled fire)

**ICP:** state forestry / fire programs, TNC & conservation NGOs, hunting preserves &
plantations (Red Hills), ranches/farms, TIMOs, land trusts. **Buyers:** Burn Boss;
Fire Program Manager; Land Manager / Steward; preserve or ranch owner.

| # | Problem | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔥 **Shrinking burn windows** — weather, humidity, air-quality, and staffing compress the days you can legally & safely burn; acres go unburned. | 10 |
| 2 | 🔥 **Escape liability** — a burn that jumps containment is catastrophic & career-ending (post-Hermits Peak/Calf Canyon, agencies & insurers are terrified). Need on-demand control of the front. | 9 |
| 3 | **Treatment backlog vs. fuel loading** — can't burn enough acres; wildfire risk compounds every missed season. | 8 |
| 4 | **Imprecise control on the finish** — drip-torch + wind guesswork; hard to steer where fire goes and stop it exactly. | 7 |
| 5 | 🔥 **Smoke management & air-quality rules** — tightening; complaints, permits, liability. | 7 |
| 6 | **Crew / labor shortage** — hard to staff qualified burn crews. | 6 |
| 7 | **No water on remote units** — hauling water is slow and costly. | 6 |
| 8 | **Permitting & public/political scrutiny** — harder after high-profile escapes. | 6 |
| 9 | **Weather turns mid-burn** — need to back the fire off fast. | 5 |
| 10 | **Insurance for private burners** — scarce and expensive. | 5 |

**Lead with:** #1 (finish more acres inside the window), #2 (control = no escape),
#4 (steer the flame front on demand). **Proof:** field test drove a flame front **~6×
faster than the adjacent control burn at only 55% output.** **Value prop:** *"Machine
control over your flame front — propagate or impede it on demand, so you finish the unit
inside the window and it stops exactly where you say."*

| 2 | **Seconds-long survivability window** in a fuel-fed aircraft fire — must knock down/redirect flame and protect egress fast. | 9 |
| 3 | 🔥 **Airfield & perimeter vegetation / WUI fire** — grass along runways, brush at the fence line, WUI encroachment; a gap foam doesn't fill. | 8 |
| 4 | **Part 139 response-time & Index compliance** — reach the farthest-runway midpoint in ~3 min; staffing/equipment to hold Index under FAA scrutiny. | 8 |
| 5 | **Fuel farm / fuel-truck fire exposure.** | 7 |
| 6 | **Water/agent supply & resupply** across a large airfield. | 7 |
| 7 | **Environmental runoff** near airport waterways/wetlands. | 6 |
| 8 | **ARFF staffing, training cost & turnover.** | 6 |
| 9 | **Mass-casualty reputational / legal exposure.** | 6 |
| 10 | **Budget constraints** (esp. regional/small hubs). | 5 |

**Lead with:** #1 (chemical-free), #3 (vegetation/WUI on the airfield — foam can't
solve it), #2 (buy the seconds that matter). **Value prop:** *"A chemical-free way to
hold and redirect fire on the airfield — from a fuel-fed flame to a grass fire along the
runway — with no foam to buy, store, or dispose of."* **(Note: airport email templates

| 5 | 🔥 **Aerial retardant restrictions & cost** — litigation/permits near water, aircraft scarcity, cost per drop. | 8 |
| 6 | **WUI expansion** — more homes in the path; defensible-space limits. | 7 |
| 7 | 🔥 **Wildland firefighter staffing/pay crisis** — retention cliff; fewer boots on the line. | 7 |
| 8 | **Water/retardant resupply logistics & cost.** | 6 |
| 9 | **Anchor points & holding line with thin resources.** | 6 |
| 10 | **Public/political & liability pressure** on cost and outcomes. | 6 |

**Lead with:** #1 (a line you hold on air alone), #2 (redirect embers off structures),
